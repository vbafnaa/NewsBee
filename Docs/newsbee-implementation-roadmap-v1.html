Newsbee / Implementation roadmap v1

Prove locally. Launch hybrid. Scale selectively.

Three stages with measurable gates. Public launch happens in Stage 2; microservices are not a prerequisite.

Product: Newsbee centrally publishes a shared weekly newspaper for each town or neighborhood. Full stories and source attribution live on its website; email previews link to them. Autonomous publishing follows editorial rules, with human review for exceptions. Aggregate local feedback tunes discretionary coverage without replacing essential news. No reader accounts or individual behavioral profiles; advertisements stay separate from editorial selection. Local-partner control is a future extension.

Terms used below: A community is a town or neighborhood served by its own weekly edition—not a separate server. A source connector (previously “adapter”) is the source-specific fetching and extraction logic used by an agent, such as a council-document connector or an events-feed connector. It is not necessarily a separate AI model or service. One connector can serve multiple locations; one location can use many connectors.

Cost estimates: USD/month for a small pilot, excluding labor, existing hardware, electricity, taxes, and paid source access. Ranges are planning allowances, not vendor quotes. Verify current plans before purchase; optional alternatives are not additive.

Stage 1 / Private POC

All-local, reproducible team environments

Generate sourced weekly editions without cloud infrastructure, hosted models, public reader data, or production email.

Build in order

  1. One end-to-end edition: one source connector → supported story → local website and email preview for one town or neighborhood.
  2. Multiple locations: reuse connectors across towns or neighborhoods; add deduplication, ongoing stories, and location-specific selection.
  3. Editorial controls: sensitive-story review, final publication checks, corrections, and separate ad slots.
  4. Repeatability: saved job state, safe retries, shared fixtures, and scheduled replay runs.
Proposed exit gate: produce a distinct edition for each of two towns or neighborhoods, using two different source connectors across the POC. Repeat for three simulated weekly cycles; each cycle produces both editions. Claims have inspectable support; seeded risk cases are held; restart and correction tests pass.

Illustrative example: a council-document connector and an events-feed connector supply Town A and Town B. Each town gets its own relevant edition. “Two connectors” means two ingestion paths—not two URLs, two models, or two services.

Stage 2 / Go to market

Local production, always-on public product

Keep production workers local. Host published stories, public intake, subscriptions, and email delivery independently.

Build in order

  1. Publish: domain, staged releases, live source links, archive, rollback, and corrections.
  2. Deliver: confirmed subscriptions, consent, unsubscribe, sender authentication, and duplicate-safe campaigns.
  3. Learn: durable feedback and ad intake; bounded aggregate topic adaptation.
  4. Pilot: monitor quality, weekly workload, availability, and cost across a small community set.
Proposed launch gate: publish one edition per week for each of two towns or neighborhoods for four consecutive weeks—eight editions total. Complete restore and correction drills; prevent duplicate campaigns in retry tests; keep intake and unsubscribe available during a 24-hour local outage. Agree quality, review-time, and spending limits before recruiting readers.

Stage 3 / Scale when justified

Independently operated services

Separate capabilities when they need different scaling, availability, access controls, or release cycles—not one service per agent or location.

Extract incrementally

  1. Acquisition: isolate slow sources and browser-worker resource needs.
  2. Publishing & delivery: independent releases, corrections, and campaign reliability.
  3. Editorial & learning: durable review waits, model routing, and community adaptation.
  4. Partner operations: scoped roles, policy inheritance, audit trails, and paid entitlements.
Extraction gate: measured bottleneck, named data owner, stable contracts, retry tests, monitoring, cost estimate, and rollback. Migrate in shadow or staged mode before transferring authoritative writes.

Keep these foundations across stages

  • Versioned records: stable story, community, evidence, edition, policy, and review IDs.
  • Safe recovery: saved progress, bounded retries, idempotent jobs, backup-restore drills.
  • Controlled publication: approve → stage → verify → publish → send; retain correction history.
  • Explicit ownership: one authoritative writer per record type; no competing local/cloud masters.
  • Editorial & privacy limits: unresolved items stay held; ads stay separate; no individual engagement profiles.

Track from day one: useful editions delivered, unsupported claims and corrections, human minutes per community, run duration, and total cost per edition.
